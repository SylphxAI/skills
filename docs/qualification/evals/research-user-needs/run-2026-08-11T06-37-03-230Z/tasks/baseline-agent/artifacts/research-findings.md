# User Research Findings — Habit-Tracking App Two-Week Dropoff

Status: draft — findings not yet loaded from session evidence
Date: 2026-08-11

## 1. Study snapshot

- Decision this research feeds: why users stop using the habit-tracking app
  after about two weeks, and what (if anything) the product should change to
  address it.
- Research question: what happens between initial adoption and dropoff around
  the two-week mark, from the user's perspective?
- Method: 6 qualitative interviews, retrospective self-report.
- Participants: 6 users of the app who stopped using it after roughly two
  weeks (recruitment details not available in this workspace).
- Scope limit: the study covers what participants recall about stopping. It
  does not cover users who stayed, users who stopped before or after the
  two-week window, or non-users.

Evidence language is limited to:

- **Observed** — directly seen in the study context;
- **Reported** — stated by participants but not directly observed;
- **Interpreted** — analytical explanation consistent with the evidence;
- **Hypothesized** — plausible account requiring more evidence.

Analysis rules that apply when loading findings:

- Build themes only after checking supporting and contradictory cases across
  all 6 sessions, not session by session.
- Preserve negative cases; contradictory segments are findings, not noise.
- Do not average contradictory needs into a single "the user" account.
- Retrospective interviews explain a changing two-week journey from memory;
  treat recalled behavior as reported, not observed. Direct observation of the
  journey (e.g., diary or longitudinal sessions) is a different evidence class.

## 4. How confident we are

Confidence is rated per finding from evidence directness, coverage,
consistency across participants, triangulation, researcher dependence, and
decision consequence — not from how quotable a comment is.

Current confidence statement:

- Confidence in any specific dropout theme: **not ratable** — no session
  evidence is loaded.
- Confidence that this study can support prevalence or causal claims: **low by
  design** — n=6, qualitative, purposive/convenience sample, self-report.
- Confidence in the session count as coverage for all material segments:
  **unknown** — recruitment matrix and stopping criteria are not documented.

Confidence would rise with: complete session ledger and locators, consistent
support across multiple sessions, disconfirming cases accounted for,
independent second-coder agreement, and coverage of material segments
(e.g., different habits, ages, contexts, access needs). It would fall with:
contradictory sessions left unexplained, leading questions in the protocol, or
analysis that only counts supporting quotes.

## 5. What we should not claim

The following claims are explicitly out of scope for this study:

- **No prevalence.** No percentage or "most users" claim. Six interviews
  cannot say how common any reason is in the user base. A prevalence question
  needs an appropriately sampled quantitative study.
- **No causation.** We cannot claim that any factor *causes* dropoff. At most,
  participants reported a reason; the reason was not manipulated or observed
  to cause the stop.
- **No population generalization.** The 6 participants are not representative
  of all users, any market segment, or "users who churn."
- **No saturation claim.** Repetition of a theme in this narrow sample is not
  saturation; do not manufacture it.
- **No behavior claims from stated reasons.** What a participant says about
  why they stopped is reported memory, not observed behavior. Memory is
  fallible, and post-hoc explanations can rationalize rather than explain.
- **No claim about users who stayed** or who stopped outside the ~two-week
  window; they were not interviewed.
- **No feature-need claims.** "Users want X" is not supported unless grounded
  in session evidence, and even then it is a need for the interviewed
  segments, not a validated demand.
- **No retention-effect claims.** There is no intervention test here; nothing
  in this study shows that changing the product would retain users.
- **No product decision is complete.** These findings (once loaded) inform
  the decision; they do not by themselves justify shipping a change.

## 6. Unresolved questions and next evidence
