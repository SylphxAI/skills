**The deep core concept — The Pulse**
Every habit has one 0–100 number: how alive it is against the plan you chose (days/week). One tap marks Done; a miss dips the pulse but never kills it; the app's deep half is the recovery arc — rescue cards with four one-tap options (make it smaller, change when, rest, archive), a 48-hour honesty window for corrections, and forecasts ("Steady by Thu if you hit 2 of 3 days"). The math is deterministic, user-computable, and property-testable; no streaks, no XP, no paid repairs, no fudged numbers.

**What's in the doc**
- **§1 Frame** — falsifiable promise (first Done ≤ 90s from cold install), benchmarked Streaks/Duolingo/Beeminder/Habitica/Habify with transfer hypotheses, six-dimension success contract.
- **§3 Flows & states** — cold start, daily check-in, miss→fade→rescue, weekly recap, reminders with fatigue guard, data/portability, and a state-authority map (pulse is always derived, never stored, so it can't disagree with history).
- **§4 Tradeoffs + rollback** — 8 deliberate tradeoffs, each with an evidence trigger and an opt-in rollback that stays on the same concept (e.g., streaks roll back to an opt-in Chain mode; sync is contract-ready, promoted if multi-device becomes a top-3 support topic). Plus a "refuse to trade" floor list.
- **§6 Acceptance criteria** — 23 testable given/when/then criteria: pulse determinism (AC-1), rollover idempotence (AC-4), correction locking with no paid path (AC-5), rest semantics (AC-6), cold-start budget (AC-8), privacy/accessibility/performance floors (AC-16–19), and rollback triggers as measurement gates (AC-20–23).
