**Verdict:** Not identified from the reported summary alone. The +14 pp (62% vs 48%, n=200/cohort) is statistically significant — 95% CI (4.3, 23.7) pp, p≈0.005 — but the cohorts differ on signup week, device, and region, so the naive contrast is confounded.

The record covers everything you asked for:
- **Estimand** — ATE on the risk-difference scale, `E[Y(1) − Y(0)]` for 7-day retention, plus the conditional `RD(w, d, r)` version the observational data can identify.
- **Assumptions** — DAG with week/device/region/unmeasured confounders; conditional exchangeability, positivity, consistency, no interference, measurement/selection integrity; and the critical caveat that if flow is a deterministic function of signup week, the effect is not identified without parallel-trends or no-concurrent-shocks assumptions.
- **Checks** — balance (SMDs), overlap, pre-trends, discontinuity at rollout, concurrent changes, negative controls, treatment/measurement integrity, missingness, model dependence.
- **Sensitivity** — E-value ≈1.9 (point), ≈1.4 (CI lower bound); bias formula `δ×γ` for a binary confounder; scenario analysis showing device/region imbalances likely contribute only ~1–2 pp, while week/time-trend confounding is the dominant threat.
- **Uncertainty** — sampling uncertainty small; identification uncertainty dominant; measurement and transportability limits.
- **What would change the conclusion** — concrete evidence paths toward causal, toward no effect, and toward not identified, plus a recommended RCT path (~200/arm for 14 pp, ~1,600/arm for 5 pp).

Bottom line for reporting: say "new-flow signups retained ~14 pp higher (associational, consistent with but not proof of a causal effect)" until the unit-level adjusted analysis and diagnostics are run.
