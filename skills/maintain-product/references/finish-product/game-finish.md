# Game Finish

Apply this reference when play, mastery, fantasy, simulation, exploration, or
social play is the primary promise. A game that runs is not necessarily a
finished game.

## Experience and art thesis

State the player fantasy, emotional tone, three to five experience pillars,
explicit design limits, and one coherent art/experience thesis. Resolve:

- silhouette, proportion, shape, scale, material, texture, palette, lighting,
  weather, atmosphere, architecture, environment, prop, character, UI, VFX,
  and camera grammar;
- composition and navigation cues across close, mid, and long distances;
- density, variation, landmark rhythm, negative space, repeated-kit visibility,
  environmental storytelling, and dead-space policy;
- animation, locomotion, body mechanics, facial or character expression,
  transitions, contact, impact, and interruption;
- music, ambience, voice, sound effects, mix hierarchy, haptics, and silence;
- quality tiers and LOD behavior that preserve identity, readability, timing,
  collision, and gameplay meaning.

Low-poly is valid only as an intentional, coherent art direction. Graybox
geometry, repeated primitives, missing materials, placeholder animation, or
default lighting are not final merely because they are internally consistent.

Finish one representative playable vertical slice before scaling content. It
should contain the approved environment, character and prop treatment, UI,
feedback, audio, camera, performance, and a complete mechanic-to-payoff loop.

## Gameplay resolution

For every selected mechanic and loop, resolve:

- player verb, input, anticipation, response, consequence, feedback, recovery,
  cancellation, chaining, mastery ceiling, and failure readability;
- teach, practice, test, remix, escalation, assistance, fair failure, retry, and
  stopping-point behavior;
- encounter composition, pacing, challenge variables, enemy/system readability,
  build or strategy diversity, dominant/degenerate strategies, exploits, and
  accessibility modes;
- moment, encounter, session, meta, progression, economy, social, and live-loop
  connections without compulsory busywork or false depth;
- content variety, recombination grammar, authored moments, repetition rate,
  reward cadence, narrative coherence, and late-entry/catch-up behavior.

Depth comes from rich, legible interaction and durable decision space. Prefer a
small verb set over a larger count of mechanics, particles, items, currencies,
quests, or screens.

## Numbers and balance

Every gameplay or economy number needs an owner, unit, valid range, curve or
distribution, rationale, affected player decision, counter-effects, and tuning
evidence. Include timing, speed, distance, health, damage, accuracy, spawn,
drop, reward, price, cost, capacity, progression, matchmaking, difficulty,
cooldown, probability, pity, decay, and reset values where applicable.

Test interactions, distributions, extremes, progression paths, dominant
strategies, exploits, inflation/sinks, new/returning/veteran cohorts, and
accessibility adjustments. A simulation establishes modeled balance behavior;
player observation establishes fun, comprehension, perceived fairness, cultural
meaning, and delight.

## Playthrough matrix

Inspect and capture representative:

- cold start, first input, first understood response, first meaningful choice,
  first failure/retry, first mastery payoff, session close, and return;
- keyboard/mouse, controller, touch, remapping, disconnect/reconnect, pause,
  suspend/resume, focus loss, and rapid/repeated input as applicable;
- low/high quality, minimum/target device, compact/wide aspect, reduced motion,
  color/contrast modes, subtitles/captions, text expansion, locale, and offline
  or degraded network states;
- quiet, dense, combat/action, traversal, menu/meta, economy, social, failure,
  and recovery scenes.

Run representative play across the selected scope; a trailer shot, one
beautiful room, or a scripted happy path covers only that captured slice.
