# App and SaaS Finish

Apply this reference to apps, SaaS, websites, and desktop products whose
primary promise is utility, creation, communication, commerce, or managed
service.

## One product story

The public promise, onboarding, product workspace, account/billing, support,
docs, status, email, notification, and integration surfaces should describe the
same product in the same domain language. Resolve:

- product and feature names, object vocabulary, action labels, statuses,
  permissions, entitlements, limits, plans, and recovery terms;
- headline, subhead, CTA, proof, pricing, comparison, onboarding, help, error,
  empty, confirmation, cancellation, support, and lifecycle copy;
- claim authority, audience, reading order, tone, specificity, localization,
  pluralization, formatting, and legal/policy ownership;
- page title, description, canonical, locale, social preview, structured data,
  sitemap, indexability, and link semantics for applicable public routes.

Every claim must be true for the product state and audience. Do not invent
testimonials, metrics, customer logos, availability, guarantees, prices,
compliance, or outcomes to make a page look complete.

## Surface finish

Inspect the applicable full denominator:

- landing, use-case, feature, pricing, comparison, content, docs, trust/legal,
  contact, and status surfaces;
- authentication, onboarding, first value, core workspace, search/navigation,
  creation/editing, collaboration/sharing, settings, account, organization,
  billing/usage, admin, support, cancellation, export, and deletion;
- loading, empty, partial, stale, permission, validation, error, offline/degraded,
  destructive, success, undo, retry, and recovery states;
- public/authenticated, mobile/desktop, keyboard/touch/pointer, compact/wide,
  long/RTL text, locale, zoom/text scaling, reduced motion, high contrast,
  assistive technology, minimum device/network, and low-end paths.

A polished landing page cannot hide a rough authenticated product. A polished
dashboard cannot compensate for broken onboarding, billing, support, or exit.

## Visual and content coherence

Resolve typography, spacing, grid, hierarchy, density, color roles, iconography,
imagery, illustration, charts, tables, forms, navigation, feedback, motion, and
responsive behavior against one product-specific direction. Use real or
representative content and realistic length, density, permissions, plans,
dates, currencies, usage, errors, and edge cases.

Default framework components may be good primitives; unchanged defaults are
not automatically a product identity. Likewise, originality is not a license
for unfamiliar controls, inaccessible contrast, gratuitous animation, or a
slower primary path.

## Quantitative product truth

Apply the shared number contract especially to:

- price, currency, billing period, tax, discount, renewal, trial, refund, and
  entitlement timing;
- limits, quotas, storage, seats, credits, units, overage, progress, completion,
  percentages, rates, ranks, analytics, dates, durations, and forecasts;
- plan comparisons, usage latency, stale/estimated values, timezone, rounding,
  threshold colors, and consequence language.

The owning commercial, billing, entitlement, analytics, or domain system
supplies truth. Finish aligns its representation and cross-surface consistency;
it does not replace that authority.

## Render and journey evidence

Capture the same representative routes and workflows before and after changes.
Include realistic fixtures and applicable viewports, inputs, locales, themes,
accessibility modes, network conditions, roles/permissions, plan states, and
failure/recovery states. Verify visual layout, interaction behavior, content
truth, metadata, accessibility, performance, and regression behavior against
the exact candidate.

Do not treat static mockups, source review, one viewport, synthetic-only text,
or a passing component test as proof that the integrated product is finished.
