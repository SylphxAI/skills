# Mechanics standard

Trigger: designing a product, a feature, an offer, or a return loop.

A design considers relevant growth and monetisation mechanisms, measures them,
and keeps those that earn their place. The [catalogue](mechanics-catalogue.md)
is a menu of candidates, not a mandatory bundle.

## Hard lines

Apply the owning standards rather than duplicating their rules:

- [Growth](growth.md#hard-lines) and [campaigns](growth.md#campaigns-and-gifts)
  own truthful promotion, consent, experiment design and campaign exposure.
- [Commercial](commercial.md) owns purchases, entitlements, billing and remedies.
- [Games](games.md#hard-lines) owns purchased value and competitive promises.
- [Risk](risk.md) owns assessment of market and store requirements, including
  those applying to a paid-randomness mechanic.

## The menu

Review the catalogue sections relevant to the decision. State the target
metrics, chosen mechanisms and material alternatives; add a new mechanism when
it better serves the product. Apply the product's actual policy and current
market/store requirements through [commercial.md](commercial.md#legal-surface)
and [risk.md](risk.md).

## How it is judged

Judge the stated outcomes after launch, including incremental retention,
conversion and contribution net of refunds and campaign cost. Use the
[experiment method](growth.md#campaigns-and-gifts) when the result can change a
decision. Report uncertainty and a material outcome/rollback rule; preserve
purchased promises. Catalogue coverage itself is not a success metric.
