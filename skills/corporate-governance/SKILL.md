# Corporate governance

Protect each legal entity through accurate records, reconciled money and verified
completion of its obligations. Work from the governing record for the specific
entity and jurisdiction, not a remembered deadline, a familiar trading name or a
prior year's spreadsheet. An internal checklist cannot prove a filing was accepted.

## Establish the entity and the governing record

Identify the exact legal name, registry number, jurisdiction, legal form and the
authorized actor for the proposed action. Match the registry record, current
constitutional documents, relevant resolutions, tax registrations and authenticated
portal account. Trading names, group branding and an email address do not identify
the contracting or taxable entity.

Read current primary sources: the official registry and tax authority, the entity's
filed records and notices, executed contracts and amendments, bank statements,
processor reports and original invoices. Retain the source, effective version or
period and read time in the entity's private records. Verify whether a register is
public, held by the entity or maintained through another prescribed route; do not
publish personal or beneficial-ownership details merely because another jurisdiction
does so. Check event-driven updates as well as recurring obligations.

If an identifier, source period, applicable rule or required authority is unreadable
or inconsistent, resolve that uncertainty before the dependent filing, payment or
notice. Continue independent reconciliations and preparation. Do not invent missing
values, treat portal absence as an exemption or turn uncertainty into a blanket halt.
Keep entity identities, account details, calendars and approval limits in caller
records, outside this reusable skill.

## Build the calendar from legal events, not familiar dates

Separate obligations that happen to share a year-end. For each entity and duty,
record the period or triggering event, review/reference date, filing window,
payment deadline if any, governing source, responsible actor and completion evidence.
Calculate only from verified inputs and the current applicable rule, including
first-period, changed-year-end, extension or exemption conditions where relevant.
Confirm the result against the authoritative portal or notice; a reminder is a
convenience, not the source of truth.

Distinguish these concepts rather than copying one date into every calendar row:

| Obligation | Verify separately |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmation statement or annual return | Review date or reporting period, information to confirm, event-driven updates, permitted filing window and acceptance |
| Statutory accounts | Accounting reference date, actual accounts period, accounting framework and filing deadline |
| Corporation tax or equivalent | Tax accounting period, payment deadline, return filing deadline and any instalment regime |
| VAT or equivalent transaction tax | Registration position, return period, filing and payment rules, applicable scheme and adjustments |
| Officer, beneficial-owner or registered-office change | Effective event, required approval, register update and notification deadline under the applicable regime |

A confirmation statement is not an accounts filing, and a tax payment deadline is
not necessarily the tax return deadline. Jurisdiction, entity type and the specific
period determine the rule; this skill supplies no universal dates or identifiers.
Verify current statutory forms, required identity checks, signatories and filing
routes before submission. Record approvals and accurate effective dates; do not
backdate records to make a missed obligation appear timely. For an overdue duty,
establish the actual status, lawful remedy and consequences from primary sources.

## Reconcile money before classifying or disputing it

Use complete statements for the relevant accounts, periods and currencies. Tie
opening balance, movements and closing balance, and match each transaction through
a one-to-one audit trail to its supporting invoice, credit note, payment or other
source and the approved spend register. Check the legal supplier, paid-account or
subscription identifier, service period, tax, currency and approval scope; an amount
or merchant descriptor alone is not enough to identify a purchase.

One-to-one traceability does not mean pretending every invoice equals one bank line.
A net processor payout can contain many orders, refunds, chargebacks, fees, reserves
and timing adjustments. Bridge those components to the payout, then match the payout
to the bank deposit. Document split or aggregated payments explicitly, retaining
source transaction IDs so nothing is counted twice. Reconcile fees, FX differences,
taxes and in-transit funds separately; do not force a match by netting unrelated
items or fabricating a balancing entry.

Investigate missing, duplicate or ambiguous lines and keep unresolved differences
visible before final classification or a dispute. Cash received is not automatically
revenue for that period; distinguish accruals, prepayments, liabilities and transfers
under the applicable accounting framework. A charge absent from the spend register
needs an approval and source check, not an automatic fraud allegation or chargeback.
Preserve original statements and receipts; corrections must remain traceable.

## Preserve legal separation between entities

Maintain the correct contracting party, bank and processor account ownership,
accounting records, tax registrations and tax position for each entity. Shared
branding or common ownership does not make their assets, liabilities or permissions
interchangeable. Confirm whose debt, service, income and authorization each
transaction represents before paying, signing or recording it.

For connected-party activity, document the real transaction and purpose, parties,
approvals and conflicts, allocation basis, arm's-length terms where applicable,
invoices or agreements, and corresponding entries in each entity's books. Consider
applicable transfer-pricing, indirect-tax, withholding and disclosure requirements
from primary rules; do not assume every intercompany transfer is an expense or a
loan. Resolve unexplained differences instead of clearing them through an arbitrary
intercompany balance. Separate records support ring-fencing but do not by themselves
guarantee legal protection; substance, conduct, guarantees and governing law matter.

## Audit recurring spend and act on contract terms

Start with the live subscription, executed agreement, amendments, order confirmation,
invoices and payment receipts. Match the paid-account identifier and contracting
entity, then verify the actual renewal date, notice window, notice recipient and
method, any cooling-off eligibility, early-termination formula and post-cancellation
obligations. A public pricing page or a different account's terms cannot establish
this agreement's rights. Calculate any exit charge from the applicable clause and
verified remaining commitment, not the latest monthly debit alone.

Compare verified use, business value, alternatives and total renewal or exit cost
with the approved spend register and the caller's change authority. Recommend the
best supported action; do not invent approval thresholds or assume authority to
cancel a dependency, surrender an account or destroy retained data. Establish
continuity, exports or recovery where the authorized action needs them.

Before sending a renewal, cancellation, dispute or escalation notice, verify the
account, facts, authority and contractual delivery route. State only supported
claims and the requested remedy. Distinguish cancellation of renewal, immediate
termination, refund request and charge dispute; one does not imply the others.
Record the actual notice, delivery evidence, effective date and final invoice or
credit, and verify subsequent subscription and billing status. Sending an email or
removing a payment method does not by itself terminate the contract.

## Close only against authoritative completion evidence

Read live status before submitting, check for an existing pending submission and
retain the operation or submission ID. A timeout with an unknown result is not
permission to file or pay twice; reconcile it with the authoritative history first.
Use [deliver-and-verify](../deliver-and-verify/SKILL.md) for external-write controls
and [prove-it](../prove-it/SKILL.md) to verify the resulting claim.

Match the registry, tax portal, bank or contractually recognized notice receipt to
the exact entity, obligation, period, submitted contents and timestamp. Verify final
acceptance or effective action, not merely upload success, an email in an inbox or
a pending acknowledgement. Payment and filing may need separate receipts; acceptance
also does not certify that every underlying accounting assertion is correct.

Keep pending, rejected and accepted states distinct. Resolve validation errors or
rejections through the normal correction route, check updated registers and balances
where applicable, and retain source documents and receipts in authorized private
records under applicable retention requirements. Close the calendar entry only when
its required outcome is verified, then derive the next obligation from the current
record. A finished draft, successful login or scheduled reminder is not completion.

For structural or commercial alternatives, use
[challenge](../challenge/SKILL.md); for dedicated correspondence after verification,
use [clear-writing](../clear-writing/SKILL.md). Neither replaces jurisdiction-specific
primary records or the authority needed for the action.
