# Sources and limitations

Read when relying on an outside claim or challenging this method. All sources
below were read on **2026-11-04**. The access column states what was actually
read; a description or abstract is not a full-book or full-paper review.
The goals, default price menu, gift delegation, asset-preservation rule and
decision thresholds are chosen operating policies, not findings proved by
these sources.

## Motivation

[Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006), *The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A
Self-Determination Theory Approach*](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf),
abstract and limitations read in the full-text copy. Across four studies, perceived autonomy and competence relate
to enjoyment and play preferences; the multiplayer survey also examines
relatedness and intended future play.

The authors identify limits in assigned laboratory play, new measures and
genre coverage. Intended future play is not a measured commercial retention
or revenue effect. This cuts against equating a longer numerical road with a
better game: test meaningful choice, competence and social value along with
observed return and contribution. Reconsider a proposed extension when those
outcomes worsen, even if it adds months of theoretical progression.

## Service recovery

[De Matos, Henrique & Rossi (2007), *Service Recovery Paradox: A
Meta-Analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1094670507303012),
[publisher-deposited abstract read through Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1094670507303012);
full paper not read. The abstract reports a significant positive cumulative
effect on satisfaction, and nonsignificant effects on repurchase intentions,
word of mouth and corporate image. It also identifies moderation by study
design, subjects and service category.

This limits any claim that a mistake plus a generous recovery makes customers
more loyal or profitable than reliable service. It does not establish zero
effect for every setting. Repair the failure, measure attention-only resolution,
and test optional gifts on actual return and purchasing. Neither provoking a
failure nor promising a revenue uplift follows from this evidence. Replace a
gift policy when comparable outcome data favor attention or another remedy.

## Experiments

| Source and access | Finding used | Consequence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kohavi, Longbotham, Sommerfield & Henne (2009), [*Controlled experiments on the web: survey and practical guide*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1); abstract, limitations, timing guidance and allocation exceptions read in the [full-text copy](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1.pdf) | Randomization supports causal attribution; power, sample size, delayed effects and assignment matter. The appendix describes market interference and customer-relation risks in price tests. | Use a credible comparison and decision-relevant effect size. Choose the allocation unit for the economy and player trust; cover complete cycles instead of copying a universal duration. |
| Johari, Pekelis & Walsh, [*Always Valid Inference: Bringing Sequential Analysis to A/B Testing*, arXiv v3 (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922v3); abstract read | Ordinary fixed-sample inference becomes unreliable when sample size is chosen by continuously monitoring significance; the paper develops always-valid inference | Preplan stopping or use an appropriate sequential procedure. Fast operational monitoring does not justify declaring an early commercial win. |

Replace an attributed method when a relevant, better-supported method improves
the decision, or when its assumptions fail. Read that replacement's primary
source and record the evidence boundary before changing the guidance.
